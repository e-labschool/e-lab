# Premium-readiness review: gases and states of matter (6 simulations)

Review date: 2026-11-16. Read-only review; no source, test or config file was changed.
Scope: `ideal-gas-simulation`, `gas-laws-explorer`, `explore-matter-and-states`, `particle-model-visualizer`, `phase-change-heating-curve`, `mixture-separation-explorer`.

## How this was done, and what it can and cannot support

- Read the engine, model and data code for all six. Read `docs/SIMULATION_AUDIT_REPORT.md`, `docs/HEATING_COOLING_CURVE.md`, `docs/STATE_PERSISTENCE.md` first so known items are not re-reported as new.
- Built the app to `/tmp/scratch/dist` and ran every simulation in headless Chromium (Playwright, SwiftShader) via `vite preview`, at 1366x768 and 390x844, with extra sizes for some. Probed: keyboard to slider min/max, rapid mode toggling and resets, resizing, reload, reduced motion, console capture. Screenshots are in `/tmp/simreview/gases/` (paths quoted per finding).
- Called the model functions directly in Node for the ideal-gas model (limits, vdW saturation, extreme states).
- Verified via web (secondary sources, not the IB PDF itself): IB 2025 S1.5 and S1.1 statements and guidance (yueyuan-org.pages.dev IB content-statement pages), and CO2 vapour pressure and critical constants (Wikipedia "Carbon dioxide (data page)"). Where a claim is from memory or reasoning, it says "reasoned".
- Not tested: real GPU performance (SwiftShader is far slower than a Chromebook GPU, so no WebGL performance conclusion is drawn), touch hardware, Firefox/Safari, screen readers, real browser zoom, colour-contrast measurements, the Learn-embedded mode. Keyboard testing was limited to range sliders.
- Known audit items are marked "(known)" where I re-confirmed them. I did not re-report the Fullscreen/New Tab controls, which the audit covers and which are present in all six.

## 1. ideal-gas-simulation ("Ideal vs Real Gas", S1.5)

**Verdict (6.5/10).** This is the most scientifically serious engine in the set. The model (`model.js`, 1148 lines) uses documented CO2 van der Waals constants, solves the gas branch properly, applies a Maxwell equal-area saturation limit, refuses to show numbers outside its validity and says why. The compressibility graph (Z vs log P) shows real physics. It handles hostile input well: no console errors across slider extremes, 160 mode toggles, reset mid-run; NaN/blank controls are sanitised in `clampControls`. The owner's "ideal gas feels weak" is mostly not about correctness. It is about what a student sees and can do: the opening state is 30 atm of CO2 (not an everyday gas), units are atm/L instead of the kPa/dm3 the IB Data Booklet uses, the piston height is a log scale with no scale shown, the pressure is not visibly produced by the particles, the only gas is CO2, and there is nothing to record or plot. It is a good real-gas demonstrator and a poor first ideal-gas lesson. It scores below 7 because a premium IB product has to teach S1.5.1 to S1.5.3 in the units the exam uses and let students gather data.

## 2. gas-laws-explorer (Boyle, Charles, Gay-Lussac, Avogadro; S1.5)

**Verdict (6/10).** Technically clean. One consistent state `{mode, P, V, T, n}` obeys PV = nRT, the dependent variable is computed, the effective slider range protects the calculated variable, and all four relationships plus their straight-line forms (P vs 1/V, V vs T in K and C, V/T flat line) are available. It survived 60 rapid n+ clicks, 20 tab switches, five viewport sizes, reload, and reduced motion with no console errors. Why it feels weak to a teacher: (a) the graph is the analytic curve drawn in advance, so the student confirms a law rather than discovers it; (b) no table, no way to take readings, no export; (c) units are atm and L and the temperature grid cannot hit 273 K or 298 K; (d) one false "limit reached" message and a few state-carry-over surprises. For a premium product, "Boyle's law" has to let a student collect (V, P) pairs, plot P vs V then P vs 1/V and see the curve straighten from their own points.

## 4. particle-model-visualizer (S1.1)

**Verdict (4.5/10).** A pleasant 3D chamber with Solid / Liquid / Gas buttons, a properties panel, Pause, Attractions and Reset View. It looks more premium than most, and has a WebGL-unavailable fallback. But it is three canned animations rather than a model: no temperature control, no energy input, no pressure or volume, no state change as a response to anything the student does. S1.1.3 (temperature measures average kinetic energy) cannot be demonstrated here. There are also physical-representation problems (below). Two caveats on my evidence: SwiftShader made WebGL slow, so a click-spam test timed out and I draw no performance conclusion; and my gas-state screenshots may have caught the fade transition (the transition time is frame-clamped), so I did not treat odd small dots in them as a defect.

## 5. phase-change-heating-curve (S1.1)

Water-only, constant power, clearly stated assumptions on screen, a "time axis to scale" option, heating and cooling, a scrub slider. No console errors across play/reset/mode spam (30 cycles). Gaps: it is a single fixed example (no mass, power, substance or Q = mc deltaT calculation), no sublimation, one known layout failure that still reproduces, and minor graph polish. This is where "keep" applies.

## Cross-cutting observations

1. **No data capture in any of the six.** There is no record button, results table, student plot or export anywhere (grep for csv/download/table/record in all six engines: nothing). For a chemistry teacher "graphs students can plot" is the main missing premium feature, most of all for Boyle/Charles/Gay-Lussac.
2. **No predict-observe-explain loop except a Predict popover** in the two gas engines (it computes the right example numbers from the model, which is good) and none in the others. There are no check questions, no challenge mode.
3. **Units and IB conventions** (kPa, dm3, 8.31, STP 273.15 K and 100 kPa, K with deg C beside it) are not followed in the gas engines.
4. **Representation drift between simulations** on the same S1.1 content (see GS-29, GS-36). A shared particle core and shared wording file would remove it.
5. **Strong foundations worth keeping:** the dashboard layout, the pure-model-plus-React split in `gas-laws-explorer` and `phase-change-heating-curve`, the honest model-limit messages in `ideal-gas-simulation`, deterministic seeded physics, existing unit tests (`test-gas-laws-model.mjs`, `test-ideal-gas-model.mjs`, `test-heating-cooling-curve.mjs`).
